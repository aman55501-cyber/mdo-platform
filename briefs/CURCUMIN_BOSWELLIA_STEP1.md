**For:** Aman · Samples and rates arrive this week. Every external number is sourced or marked **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## Which to quote: finished capsule or extract powder?
**Extract powder.** Reasons: (1) your samples are powder; (2) a powder is an *ingredient* sold B2B to supplement brands, contract
manufacturers and distributors, who hold the brand, the label claims and the US regulatory burden for the finished product;
(3) capsules make you a brand: label law, claims, retail, returns, marketing spend, a much bigger business than you need to start;
(4) long-term USA buyers (your goal) buy ingredient on specification plus certificate of analysis, repeat monthly or quarterly.
Capsules can come later as private label for a buyer who asks.

**Quality risk to design around:** turmeric has a history of lead chromate adulteration for colour; FDA issued an import alert after 2013 recalls and
recalls continued in 2016 ([Food Safety Tech](https://foodsafetytech.com/tag/lead-chromate/), [ASTA](https://astaspice.org/?p=279)). US buyers will ask
for lead results and supply-chain traceability. A clean, third-party lead test on every lot is a selling point, not a cost.

## Sample plan (when they arrive)
1. Send each sample to an **independent NABL-accredited lab**: HPLC curcuminoids / AKBA, lead-arsenic-cadmium-mercury, pesticides, residual solvents, microbials. Compare with the supplier's COA. A mismatch = disqualify.
2. Ask each supplier for: FSSAI licence, GMP/ISO 22000/FSSC certificate, FDA food-facility registration number (needed to ship to the USA), yield and MOQ, lead time, payment terms, price at 25 / 100 / 500 kg.
3. Keep 100 g of each sample sealed as a retain.

## Cost map — internal and external (fill with rates when they arrive)
**Internal (per kg of finished extract):** raw turmeric rhizome / resin (kg needed per kg ÷ yield) · extraction solvent and recovery loss · processing / toll fee if outsourced · drying and milling · QC lab per lot · packaging (food-grade drum, liner) · labour and utilities · wastage and rejected lots · working capital for stock and receivables · overhead share.
**External (to land at a US buyer):** export packing and documentation · freight (air for samples and first lots, sea for volume) and insurance · Indian export certificates (IEC, FSSAI export NOC if asked, phytosanitary / certificate of origin when requested) · US customs duty on the HTS line (**[UNVERIFIED]** — get the line and rate from a customs broker) · US importer of record or FSVP agent (usually the buyer; check) · FDA registration and prior notice · third-party testing and certifications buyers ask for (Kosher, Halal, Non-GMO, organic optional) · product-liability insurance (buyers ask for a certificate) · broker / distributor commission · payment terms (net 30–60 days) and FX.
Output of the cost map: landed cost per kg at three volumes, the minimum price you can quote, and the gross margin at the market price.
